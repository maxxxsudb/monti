(F) Exclusion outcome kills the mirror. Consider a test of a single door with finitely many outcomes, all with positive probability off-target, one of which is an exclusion outcome (impossible at the target: likelihood ratio 0; for a coin host, "the door is opened"). All rivals are already tested, priors are arbitrary, payoffs are car +1, goat -g (g > 0), walk away 0. Theorem: if testing your door (for the coin host: lifting the protection) has positive value under forced choice, it also has positive value when walking away is allowed. Hence, for Monty-type hosts, there is no "mirror" of the walk-away effect (useful under forced choice but worthless with walk-away). Without an exclusion outcome such mirrors exist (e.g. a noisy binary test with priors 1/7, 3/7, 3/7: +5/108 under forced choice, exactly 0 with walk-away, although playing is profitable).
