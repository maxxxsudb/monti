RESEARCH TASK: invent a simple, humanly counterintuitive, potentially previously unknown probability paradox from a generalized Monty Hall mechanism. Do constructive discovery, not a survey of familiar puzzles.

Core model: a hidden target/state X, an informed or constrained filter, a protected choice that does not face the same filter, and an observation history. Two options can have the same visible present but different histories. Move the viewpoint/boundary: what counts as the object versus its support/controller; what is protected versus selected at different stages; the boundary between reading an already fixed outcome and causally creating/changing the future; or the boundary between a local snapshot and the relevant larger system. The breakthrough should arise from this boundary shift, not from plugging a new probability into the usual doors.

Propose THREE genuinely different concrete candidate mechanisms. For each: (1) a school-level puzzle in at most 3 sentences, fully specified; (2) ONE exact numerical answer with a short calculation; (3) the plausible human intuitive answer, labelled a hypothesis rather than measured; (4) explain the selective filter/protection and exactly which boundary shift creates the surprise; (5) strongest known-paradox reduction or concrete published predecessor, with an explicit mapping if one exists. If a candidate fails, improve the mechanism rather than relabelling the same old puzzle.

Avoid merely n-door Monty, Monty Fall/Crawl/biased-host variants, three prisoners, Bertrand boxes, boy-girl/Tuesday boy, Berkson/collider, survivorship/inspection, Simpson/Braess/Lindley, Sleeping Beauty, envelopes, Parrondo, friendship, winner curse, two witnesses, Newcomb, optional stopping, base-rate fallacy or selected retesting. Also avoid mixing old game types, simple damage/destruction of a prize after a reveal, merely flipping good/bad labels, or arbitrary random true statements/divisors. These have already failed our search.

Do NOT certify novelty from not remembering a name. But saying “it is Bayes/CAR/decision theory” is also not a sufficient rejection: every probability puzzle uses general mathematics. Distinguish a generic framework from a concrete equivalent mechanism. Models being able to solve a puzzle does not imply humans find it intuitive. Seek a structural new effect, a short story and a single small exact fraction. Prioritize actual candidate construction.
